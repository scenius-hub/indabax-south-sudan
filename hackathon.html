The Challenge: Can you predict food insecurity risk?

South Sudan's food security is assessed using the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC), which periodically classifies each county's severity from Minimal to Catastrophe. As of the most recent analysis, 7.7 million people (57% of South Sudan's population) were facing Crisis-or-worse food insecurity — driven by conflict, low agricultural production, economic shocks, and climate events like flooding.

But formal IPC classifications only happen a few times a year and require extensive analysis by food security experts. Between assessment cycles, humanitarian and government responders need faster ways to identify counties whose situation may be deteriorating.

Your task: build a model that predicts whether a South Sudanese county will experience Crisis-or-worse food insecurity (IPC Phase 3+) during the current assessment period — using only information available before that period, including:

  • The county's population
  • The year and month of the assessment period
  • The county's IPC classification during its previous assessment
  • The percentage of the county's population previously classified in IPC Phase 3 or above
  • The county's most recently available cereal production figures
  • The county's most recently available cereal production gap

This is a genuine forecasting challenge — all features represent information that would have been available before the target assessment period, with no current-period IPC information included in the inputs. A successful solution could help humanitarian organisations and government agencies identify counties requiring closer assessment and prioritize limited field and response resources. The model is intended to complement the IPC's expert-led process — not replace it.

How It's Scored

Submissions are evaluated on AUC (Area Under the Curve) — how well your model distinguishes counties at Crisis-or-worse risk from those that aren't.

Target variable: food_insecurity_risk

  • 1 = the county is in Crisis, Emergency, or Catastrophe (IPC Phase 3+)
  • 0 = the county is below Crisis level (IPC Phase 1 or 2)

Zindi maintains a public leaderboard (≈30% of the test dataset) during the challenge, and a private leaderboard (the remaining 70%) that determines final rankings once the challenge closes.

Hackathon Guidelines & Eligibility

Who Can Participate

  • Open to All: This challenge is open to new users on Zindi who meet the challenge eligibility requirements. All experience levels are welcome.
  • Team Size: Compete individually or in teams of up to 4 members.

Rules at a Glance

  • Open-source languages, packages, and tools only — no AutoML pipelines that auto-generate full modelling solutions.
  • Only the provided challenge data may be used — no external datasets.
  • Maximum 10 submissions per day, 300 for the entire challenge.
  • Before the challenge closes, you must select two submissions for private leaderboard evaluation.
  • Top 10 finishers on the private leaderboard must submit reproducible code within 48 hours of being requested, along with a short write-up (features used, validation strategy, chosen decision threshold with F1/precision/recall) and a presentation at the Showcase.
  • Challenge data is available under a CC BY-SA 4.0 licence.

Full official rules are on the Zindi challenge page.
